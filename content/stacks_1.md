# Stacks

A stack is a linear data structure that follows a particular order for adding and removing elements. The principle it follows is Last-In, First-Out (LIFO). 

### The LIFO Principle

Think of a stack of plates in a cafeteria. When you add a new plate, you place it on top. When someone takes a plate, they take it from the top. The last plate that was placed on the stack is the first one to be removed. In computer science, this same concept is applied to data.

### Push and Pop

The two primary operations on a stack are Push and Pop. "Push" adds an item to the top of the stack. "Pop" removes the item from the top of the stack. Attempting to pop from an empty stack results in a stack underflow error.

### Applications

Stacks are used in many places in computing. The "Undo" feature in your text editor uses a stack to remember your previous actions. Web browsers use a stack to keep track of the pages you've visited, allowing you to use the "Back" button.
